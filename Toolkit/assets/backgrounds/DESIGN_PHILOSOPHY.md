# Tango Aurora

*The visual language of the DM Toolkit screens, built on the Tavant brand: Tango #F36F26, black and white.*

Tango Aurora shows data as light. Each screen gets one luminous scene. Warm glows of Tango, amber and coral gather behind glass. Frosted cards hold the objects of the work, such as files, databases, clouds, fields and tests. Ribbons of light carry records between them. The mood is confident and optimistic: a migration that is under control and moving. Every scene should look meticulously crafted, like the hero illustration of a world-class product launch, polished pixel by pixel by someone at the top of their field.

**Colour and light.** Tango is the heartbeat, amber adds warmth and coral adds energy. Black and white give structure. Colour arrives as light, never as flat paint. Soft aurora pools are large, blurred and layered, so the background feels lit rather than coloured. Strokes glow with a halo of their own hue, and every ribbon has a bright white core, as if it carried current. The same scene exists twice: on warm cream paper for the day theme, and on Tavant black at night, where the glow becomes neon. Each tint is chosen with painstaking care so that the two versions feel like one design.

**Form and material.** Glass is the one material. Cards have generous rounded corners, a frosted body, a bright rim and a soft warm shadow, and they float above the aurora in quiet depth. The glyphs inside them are drawn geometrically, cleanly and simply, so they stay readable at a glance and never tip into clip-art. Orbs are the focal points: glossy spheres with a specular highlight and a halo. Curves are smooth cubic arcs that are never jagged. Rings, dashes and particle trails add rhythm and a sense of motion.

**Composition.** The scene lives on the right and along the top. The lower left dissolves into a soft wash, because that is where the application's content sits. Hierarchy comes from light: the brightest orb is the moment that matters on that screen, the ribbons lead the eye to it, and everything else supports it. Nothing overlaps awkwardly, nothing crowds an edge, and every card has room to breathe.

**Craft.** Master-level execution is the standard. Lines meet exactly, glows never smear into mud, and spacing follows a rhythm. A scene should hold up as a full-screen marketing visual and still sit gracefully behind a translucent panel of forms and tables. When a scene needs more, the answer is to refine the light, spacing and balance of what is there, not to add more objects.
